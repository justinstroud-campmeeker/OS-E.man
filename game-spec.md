# Summanry

This is an "idle-style" game that is meant to run in the lower portion of the user's screen. It is meant to be unobtrusive and not terribly demanding. Most interactions can be done within the game's universal terminal interface nodes.

The player is meant to represent a DevOps engineer. They are versed in Python and some shell scripting. They work for a faceless consulting behemoth, and most of their projects are in the retail PoS sector.

As the game progresses, the player becomes more and more powerful through access to systems, digital impersonation to facilitate social engineering, real-world interactions such as obtaining the necessary digital authorization to manage command and control systems for public utilities, law enforcement, communications, and military technology.

Eventually, the player becomes so powerful that they are the de-facto 'shadow emperor' of planet earth. The goal at that point is to maintain anonymity.

While all of this is happening, the player still comes to work every day. His boss doesn't respect him (or her) or their time, coworkers tease him relentlessly, and they live a lonely and boring life outside of the office. Their alter ego, however, is spoken of in hushed tones. They are feared and nobody dares to speak/stand against them.

# Background

The game is set within the context of the player's place of employment.

Tasks for the player should start small and resemble a "coding challenge refresher." It will be a little bit of python, a little bit of ANSI SQL. The reason the player is doing it is because they receive an email from their employer requiring their participation in the training.

As the training progresses, the commands become more invasive. The SQL becomes more apt to return sensitive information from within. The presentation of these anomalies should be subtle at first, but not punishingly so.

The player may choose to poke around in this sensitive data, or they may choose to continue the mundane "challenge" training. If they do, the events presenting back doors and sensitive data become more frequent and marginally more obvious.

As the player dives in to this restricted and empowering content, they unlock abilities such as the ability to blackmail NPCs into giving them more access or abilities, automating said blackmail, creating front organizations, manipulating the mass media, employing hapless conspiracy theorists and revelotionary factions to do their real-world bidding, etc.

# Tools Available to the Player
Most interaction is done through the terminal. The player's character is notorious for eschewing GUI tools in favor of the TUI. Tools include:

- A stripped down shell called 'crab' (this is their primary mode of interaction)
- A small subset of *nix commands.
  - OS/FS/net related
    - ls
    - cat
    - man (for commands and tutorial help)
    - netstat
    - ipconfig
    - whois
    - ping
  - Scripting
    - The player uses a heavily sandboxed and stripped down version of python called 'snek'.
- A simple email client called 'emmy'
- A badly implemented LLM called 'grog'
- dingaling (a crappy terminal chat client used globally)
- bledit (a simplistic text editr similar to pico)
- boardz (an interface to abusenet, similar to usenet...in this universe, it's like google, newsgroups, news aggregators, dating sites all rolled into one service. Basically all the web/content parts of the internet as well but HEAVILY simplified.)

# Stack
Use python, and a good TUI library that allows CSS and the like.
